What we do

Scopes we
self-perform

Reinforcing steel is all we do. That means our foremen read the drawings, our crews know the bar list before they get on site, and nobody is learning your job on your schedule. See these scopes on real jobs in the portfolio, or send drawings for a number.

01 — Core scope

Rebar Installation

Placing and tying reinforcing steel to print — footings, grade beams, slabs on grade, walls, columns, pier caps and mat foundations.

  • Placed to drawingBar size, spacing, laps and cover set from the structural drawings and the bar list — not from memory.
  • Supported properlyChairs, bolsters and slab bolsters sized so the mat holds its elevation under boots and buggies.
  • Tied to holdIntersections tied at the frequency the spec calls for, so nothing walks during the pour.
  • Ready for inspectionWe walk it with the inspector and fix what's called before the truck shows up.

02 — Core scope

Tie-In

Joining new steel to what's already in the ground. Dowels, laps, splices and cold joints — the connections that decide whether two pours behave like one structure.

  • Lap lengths heldWe hold the lap schedule. Short laps are the cheapest problem to cause and the most expensive to fix.
  • Dowel layoutDowels set to template and secured so they're still where the print put them when the forms come off.
  • Existing conditionsField-verified before we start. If what's in the ground doesn't match the drawing, you hear it from us that day.
  • Clean jointsSteel kept clear of the joint line so the next pour bonds the way the engineer drew it.

03 — Core scope

Tilt-Up

Panel reinforcing on the casting slab, built to the erection drawings and ready for the crane on the day the schedule says.

  • Panel mats & trimField mat, trim bar around openings, and corner reinforcement placed per the panel drawings.
  • Lift & brace insertsInserts set to the engineered layout and tied off so nothing shifts under the finishers.
  • Embeds & weld platesLocated, secured and checked against the panel elevation before the pour.
  • Crane-day readyPanels sequenced to match the erection order so the crane never waits on steel.

04 — Core scope

Elevated Decks

Top steel, bottom steel and everything between — on composite metal deck and formed slabs, worked safely at height.

  • Two-way matsBottom and top mats placed and supported so the top steel stays top steel after the pour crew walks it.
  • Column & shear zonesAdditional bars, studs and shear reinforcement at columns and drops, placed to the framing plan.
  • Tied off, all day100% tie-off at height. Leading edges flagged, holes covered, deck kept clear.
  • Pour-rate readyDeck loaded and staged so the mat gets tied ahead of the concrete, not behind it.
